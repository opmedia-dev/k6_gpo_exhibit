999 HISTORY — The World's First Emergency Number
=================================================
File: /history/999_history.mp3
Estimated duration: ~45 seconds

--- SCRIPT ---

Britain's nine-nine-nine emergency number was the world's first, introduced on the thirtieth of June, nineteen thirty-seven.

The idea came after a tragic fire in London, where five women died partly because neighbours struggled to reach the fire brigade by telephone. The GPO responded by creating a single, easy-to-remember number that would connect callers to police, fire, or ambulance services immediately.

The number nine was chosen deliberately. On a rotary dial, nine is the furthest hole from the finger stop, making it easy to find in the dark or in thick smoke simply by feel. Dialling three nines also gave the exchange equipment enough time to set up the priority connection.

The very first nine-nine-nine call was made in London on the first of July, nineteen thirty-seven. Within a week, over a thousand emergency calls had been received.

Today, nine-nine-nine handles over thirty million calls a year across the United Kingdom.
