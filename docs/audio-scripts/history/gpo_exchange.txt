GPO EXCHANGE — How Telephone Calls Were Connected
==================================================
File: /history/gpo_exchange.mp3
Estimated duration: ~50 seconds

--- SCRIPT ---

Before automatic exchanges, every telephone call in Britain was connected by hand.

When you lifted the receiver, a GPO operator would answer and ask, "Number please?" You would tell them the number you wanted, and they would physically connect your call by plugging a cable into the correct socket on a large switchboard.

At busy exchanges, hundreds of operators — most of them women — sat in long rows, handling thousands of calls every hour. It was skilled, fast-paced work that required patience and a good memory.

The first automatic exchanges began appearing in the nineteen twenties, allowing callers to dial numbers directly. By the nineteen sixties, most of Britain had switched to automatic dialling, and the era of the telephone operator gradually came to an end.

But for many years, dialling one hundred would still connect you to an operator — a service that remained available well into the nineteen nineties.
