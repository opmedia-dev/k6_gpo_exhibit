K6 HISTORY — The Story of the K6 Telephone Box
================================================
File: /history/k6_history.mp3

--- SCRIPT ---

The iconic K6 telephone box is one of Britain's most recognisable landmarks.

Designed by architect Sir Giles Gilbert Scott, the K6 was introduced in 1935 to mark the Silver Jubilee of King George the Fifth. Its distinctive red colour made it easy to spot in towns, villages, and cities across the country.

More affordable than earlier designs, over sixty thousand K6 kiosks were installed, becoming a familiar part of everyday life. For decades, they connected communities, allowing people to make calls long before mobile phones existed.

As technology evolved, many telephone boxes disappeared, but thousands of K6 kiosks survive today. Some still serve as public telephones, while others have been transformed into libraries, defibrillator stations, and community information points.

Nearly a century after its introduction, the K6 remains a lasting symbol of British design, innovation, and public service.
