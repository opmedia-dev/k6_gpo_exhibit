BUTTON A AND BUTTON B — How Public Telephones Worked
=====================================================
File: /history/button_ab.mp3
Estimated duration: ~45 seconds

--- SCRIPT ---

Making a call from a public telephone box wasn't quite as simple as picking up the phone at home.

First, you lifted the handset and listened for the dial tone. Then you inserted your coins — usually pennies or later, new pence — into the slot at the top. Next, you dialled the number you wanted.

When the person at the other end answered, you would hear them speak, but they couldn't hear you — not until you pressed Button A. This released your coins into the collection box and connected your voice to the line.

But what if nobody answered, or you dialled the wrong number? That's where Button B came in. Pressing Button B returned your coins to the cup at the bottom, so you could try again or take your money back.

Many children knew a useful trick: always check the Button B cup before making a call. The previous caller might have forgotten to collect their change.
