SPEAKING CLOCK — The Story of TIM
==================================
File: /history/speaking_clock.mp3
Estimated duration: ~45 seconds

--- SCRIPT ---

"At the third stroke, the time will be..."

The Speaking Clock, affectionately known as TIM, was introduced by the GPO in nineteen thirty-six. It quickly became one of the most-dialled numbers in Britain, with millions of calls every year.

To hear the time, you simply dialled one-two-three. A recorded voice would announce the precise time, followed by three short pips to mark the exact second.

The original voice belonged to Ethel Cain, a telephonist from Croydon, who won a competition to become the "Girl with the Golden Voice." Her recordings served callers for twenty-seven years.

At its peak, the Speaking Clock received over two hundred and fifty million calls a year. Even today, you can still dial one-two-three to hear the time — though far fewer people do now that everyone carries a clock in their pocket.
